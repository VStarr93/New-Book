# The Shark Nobody Would Sit By

First draft. Change anything that does not sound right.

Gerald was a hammerhead shark, and he was the only shark in his elementary school.

The rest of the class were fish. Jeremiah and Donald were clownfish. Jeffrey was a goldfish, the only goldfish in the room. Their teacher was an ocean sunfish. His name was Teacher. Everybody else stayed in a bunch, and nobody sat by Gerald.

They thought he was going to eat them all.

Gerald did not eat them. He came to school. He sat at his desk. He waited for someone to talk to him. Jeremiah would not. Donald would not. The other fish would not. They kept their eyes on him and kept their distance.

It was not fair. He was right there. All they had to do was talk.

When school ended, Gerald swam home. The water felt bigger when nobody was in it with him. He got into bed sad, because no one wanted to be his friend.

The next morning he went to school, and it was the same as always. Desks filled up. A gap stayed open on either side of Gerald. No one wanted to sit by him.

Jeffrey looked at that gap. He knew it. He was the only goldfish in the classroom, and some days that made the room feel like it had no place for him either.

Jeffrey swam up to Gerald.

"Hi," Jeffrey said.

Gerald blinked. Hammerheads have wide-set eyes, and both of them turned toward the small goldfish.

"Hi," Gerald said.

"Do you want to be friends?" Jeffrey asked.

"Yes," Gerald said. "Nobody else will."

They talked. They talked about being friends. Gerald was big. His head stretched out to both sides, and his shadow covered a whole desk. Jeffrey was small. Next to Gerald, Jeffrey looked like a bright coin.

"You are really big," Jeffrey said.

"You are really small," Gerald said.

They both laughed, and the gap beside Gerald was gone, because Jeffrey was in it.

Jeremiah and Donald still watched. The other fish still thought a shark might eat them. Jeffrey stayed anyway.

When the day was over, Gerald and Jeffrey did not go back to their own separate homes and wait for another morning like the last one. They left school behind. They swam out of the elementary school and into the ocean, side by side, one very big and one very small.

They kept talking. Out there, nobody had to decide whether to sit by Gerald. Jeffrey already had.

They became the bestest of friends.
